# Minecraft strategy guide

Optional expert knowledge for Minecraft Java 26.1 survival. These are option menus with tradeoffs, not orders: compare them with your fresh facts and choose. Nothing here is an observation of your world; locations still have to be found and checked.

## Getting ores and resources

Ranked by usual time per useful ore:

1. **Exposed ore.** Cave walls, ravines, mountain cliffs, dripstone and lush caves show ore without digging. Look around at entrances, junctions and walls before mining blind; one cave visit often yields more iron and coal than an hour of tunnelling.
2. **Structure loot.** Chests hold ingots, tools and sometimes diamonds already processed (see topic structures).
3. **Flooded and aquifer caves.** Underwater cave sections are often unlooted ore walls. Water costs air, not health (see topic water).
4. **Branch or strip mining.** Predictable but slow and luck-based: every block you mine reveals only its neighbours. Use it when no cave or structure is known nearby, or for diamonds deep down. Two-high tunnels with two or three solid blocks between them reveal the most per block mined.

Ore heights (overworld, 26.1 generation):

| Ore | Range | Best places |
|---|---|---|
| Coal | Y 0 to 320 | Around Y 96 and exposed on mountains |
| Copper | Y -16 to 112 | Around Y 48; dripstone caves have extra |
| Iron | Y -64 to 72 and Y 80 to 320 | Around Y 16 underground, and high in mountains around Y 232; very common in cave walls |
| Gold | Y -64 to 32 | Around Y -16; badlands also near the surface |
| Lapis | Y -64 to 64 | Around Y 0 |
| Redstone | Y -64 to 15 | Increases toward Y -59 |
| Diamond | Y -64 to 16 | Increases toward Y -59; diamonds touching air are often skipped by generation, so deep tunnels find more than cave walls |
| Emerald | Mountains only | High in mountain biomes |
| Ancient debris | Nether Y 8 to 119 | Around Y 15 |

Large ore veins: iron veins with tuff in deepslate (Y -60 to -8) and copper veins with granite (Y 0 to 50) give many ores at once.

Deep warning: below about Y -54 underground lakes and aquifers are lava, not water. Diamond level is lava-pool level. Watch the blocks ahead and below, carry a water bucket or spare blocks, and never dig straight down without checking under your feet.

Typical early amounts: iron pickaxe 3, bucket 3, shield 1, flint and steel 1, full iron armor 24. Diamond pickaxe 3 diamonds.

## Structures and loot

| Structure | Where | Useful for |
|---|---|---|
| Village | Plains, desert, savanna, taiga, snowy plains | Blacksmith, armorer, weaponsmith and toolsmith chests (iron, tools, armor, sometimes diamonds and obsidian); hay bales for bread; beds; iron golems drop iron; trades |
| Shipwreck | Oceans and beaches | Treasure chest (iron, gold, emeralds, sometimes diamonds); supply chest (food); map chest with buried treasure maps |
| Buried treasure | Beaches, via a treasure map | Iron, gold, often diamonds, TNT, heart of the sea |
| Ruined portal | Any dimension | Obsidian toward a nether portal, flint and steel or fire charge, gold gear, sometimes golden apples; often next to lava |
| Mineshaft | Underground, often joined to caves; badlands near the surface | Chest minecarts (iron, gold, bread, sometimes diamonds), rails, ore exposed along tunnels; cobwebs and cave spider spawners |
| Desert temple | Desert | Four chests under the floor (gold, iron, emeralds, sometimes diamonds); a pressure plate on TNT in the center |
| Trial chamber | Underground, copper and tuff rooms | Vault loot with trial keys; many mob spawners |
| Ancient city | Deep dark, around Y -51 | Rich loot; sculk sensors summon the warden, so sneak and avoid noise |

Seeing a structure from a distance and taking its chests usually costs less time than mining the same materials.

## Water and swimming

Water is not a hazard like lava. Swimming costs air, not health; drowning damage starts only when air reaches zero.

- Air is 300 ticks, 15 seconds under water. Observe reports underWater and airSecondsLeft, and an air warning reaches you at half air. Air refills to full in about 4 seconds with your eyes above water.
- navigateTo swims at the surface and through flooded tunnels, shafts and caves while one breath covers the underwater stretch, roughly 24 blocks with a 3 second reserve. It waits at the surface to catch breath before a long dive, rises for air when breath runs short, and can end standing on the floor of flooded space. For a longer flooded passage, choose legs between air pockets.
- Control input in water: jump held rises or stays afloat; releasing it sinks; sprint while underWater swims along the view pitch.
- Mining with your head under water is about 5 times slower, and again about 5 times slower while floating instead of standing. Stand on the floor before mining. A helmet with Aqua Affinity removes the water penalty; Respiration extends breath.
- Breaking into water while tunnelling: flowing water pushes you but does no damage. Swim through it, block the flow with a placed block, or pick up the source with a bucket. A flooded cave is often an unlooted ore wall.
- Placing a door under water clears an air pocket you can breathe in. Soul sand bubble columns push you up and refill air; magma bubble columns pull you down.
- Water against a lava source makes obsidian; against flowing lava, cobblestone. Pouring water over lava makes it crossable. A water bucket placed just before landing cancels fall damage. Water cannot be placed in the Nether.
- Boats cross open water fast with no air use. Drowned (sometimes with tridents) and guardians are the main water threats.

## Survival basics

- Food first: kill passive animals and cook the meat, or make bread from 3 wheat (village hay bales give 9 wheat each). Saturated food lets health regenerate.
- A shield (6 planks and 1 iron) blocks arrows, melee and creeper blasts. Early iron armor is a big safety gain.
- Carry a water bucket for falls, fire and lava, and a stack of blocks to bridge, pillar or wall off mobs and lava.
- Sleep in a bed at night to skip it and set your spawn. Light caves and bases with torches to stop mob spawns.
- Do not dig straight down or up under gravel and sand. Check for lava below and ahead, especially below Y -54.
- Lava: hold your distance, block the flow, or turn the source into obsidian with water. Fire resistance potions make lava survivable.

## Beating the game

Beating the game means killing the ender dragon. One common route, with alternatives at each step:

1. **Start:** wood, a crafting table and stone tools; food; a bed if wool is easy.
2. **Iron:** exposed cave iron, village chests, shipwrecks, buried treasure, iron golems, or mining around Y 16. Aim for a bucket, flint and steel, an iron pickaxe and a shield; armor if time allows.
3. **Nether portal** (see topic nether): cast obsidian in place with a water bucket over a lava pool, complete a ruined portal, or mine 10 obsidian with a diamond pickaxe.
4. **Blaze rods:** about 6 or 7 from fortress blazes (see topic nether).
5. **Ender pearls:** about 12 to 16. Barter gold with piglins, hunt endermen (warped forests in the Nether are full of them; overworld nights), or trade with a cleric villager.
6. **Eyes of ender:** 1 blaze powder and 1 ender pearl each. Locate the stronghold and fill the portal (see topic end).
7. **Dragon fight:** see topic end.

Typical time savers: loot structures instead of mining, travel long overworld distances through the Nether, and carry blocks, food, a water bucket and a bow into the End.

## The Nether

- **Portal frame:** obsidian 4 wide and 5 tall; the corners are optional, so 10 obsidian is enough. Light it with flint and steel or a fire charge. With one water bucket you can cast each obsidian block in place by pouring water over lava source blocks in a mould.
- **Travel:** 1 block in the Nether is 8 blocks in the overworld. A portal built in the Nether at overworld coordinates divided by 8 links to that area.
- **Fortress:** dark nether brick bridges and corridors. Blazes from spawners drop blaze rods; wither skeletons; nether wart; chests may hold diamonds, iron, gold and obsidian. Fortresses run in long north-south or east-west lines.
- **Bastion remnant:** blackstone ruins full of piglins and gold blocks; chests with gold, obsidian, crying obsidian and gear. Piglin brutes always attack.
- **Piglins:** wear at least one gold armor piece or they attack. Opening chests or mining gold near them angers them. Dropping gold ingots near a calm piglin barters; ender pearls (2 to 4) come back on roughly 1 in 45 trades, so plan on many gold ingots; other returns include obsidian, fire resistance potions, string and more.
- **Dangers:** lava seas and hidden lava pockets, ghast fireballs (hit them back, or use cover), falls. Water evaporates here. Beds explode when used here.

## The End

- **Finding the stronghold:** throw an eye of ender; it flies toward the stronghold and sometimes breaks. Note the direction, move a few hundred blocks sideways, throw again and intersect the two lines, or follow eyes. The nearest strongholds are 1,280 to 2,816 blocks from the world origin.
- **Portal room:** 12 frame blocks; some may already hold eyes. Silverfish spawner on the stairs. Count the empty frames before crafting more eyes.
- **Dragon fight options:**
  - Destroy the end crystals on top of the obsidian pillars first (arrows, or pillar up to the caged ones); they heal the dragon.
  - Melee the dragon when it perches on the central fountain, or shoot it with a bow while it flies.
  - Beds explode when used in the End. A bed set off next to the perched dragon does heavy damage, and heavy damage to you if you stand close; this is a fast but risky option.
- **End safety:** do not look endermen in the eyes (a carved pumpkin on your head prevents it, at the cost of vision). Dragon breath and knockback push players into the void; stay away from the edges, and keep blocks and a water bucket to break falls.
